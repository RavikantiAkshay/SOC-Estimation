Enhanced Feature Space Expansion (7-Feature Optimal Set)

While the 4-feature baseline provides a solid electro-thermal foundation, pure instantaneous electrical measurements miss critical dynamic powertrain and kinematic state context. To maximize SOC estimation accuracy strictly within the Random Forest paradigm without modifying tree hyperparameters or split mechanics, we evaluated the universal telemetry channels and identified 3 high-impact powertrain and kinematic features to augment the baseline observables:

# Feature Name Units Domain Category Physical Function & Justification
1 Battery Voltage V Baseline Observable Direct proxy for cell open-circuit voltage ($V_{\text{oc}}$); primary electrochemical driver of SOC throughout discharge and charge phases.
2 Battery Current A Baseline Observable Instantaneous electrical load; governs dynamic $I \cdot R$ polarization drop and instantaneous charge flux across the terminals.
3 Battery Temperature °C Baseline Observable Internal pack temperature; directly modulates Lithium-ion internal resistance ($R_{\text{int}}$) and chemical reaction kinetics.
4 Ambient Temperature °C Baseline Observable External environmental thermal boundary; dictates convective and conductive heat transfer between the battery pack and outside air.
5 Throttle % Powertrain Intent Driver accelerator pedal demand ($0\text{--}100\%$). Anticipates mechanical load spikes fractions of a second before electrochemical battery current surges manifest, signaling impending power draws or coasting transitions.
6 Motor Torque Nm Powertrain Work Instantaneous electromagnetic shaft torque produced or absorbed by the electric motor. Directly couples battery electrical power to mechanical drivetrain tractive effort, distinguishing motive propulsion from auxiliary loads.
7 Velocity km/h Vehicle Kinematics Instantaneous road speed. Disentangles high-speed sustained aerodynamic drag regimes (highway driving, steady continuous discharge) from low-speed urban stop-and-go cycles (intermittent pulse discharge and frequent regenerative charging).
Physical Synergy of the 7-Feature Configuration:
By uniting the 4 electro-thermal observables with 3 powertrain dynamics (Throttle, Motor Torque, Velocity), the Random Forest acquires complete physical visibility into driver intent, mechanical conversion, and vehicle kinetic energy regimes. Crucially, feature importance analysis confirms that Battery Voltage remains the dominant split driver (57.95% MDI), while the 3 powertrain features contribute a collective 9.03% to refine predictions during dynamic transients, reducing test RMSE by over 22.3%.

5. Step 5 — Model Configuration & Hyperparameters

Configures the Random Forest regressor with hyperparameters optimized for noisy battery telemetry, aligning with MATLAB's TreeBagger regression architecture.

Implementation

  • Module: config.py → RF_BASE_PARAMS
  • Library: scikit-learn → RandomForestRegressor

Hyperparameter Configuration

Parameter Value Reasoning
n_estimators 25, 50, 75, 100 4 configurations evaluated; best selected empirically
criterion squared_error Standard MSE-based split criterion for regression
max_features sqrt At each split, only √4 = 2 features are considered. This de-correlates trees and prevents Battery Voltage from dominating every split
min_samples_leaf 5 Each leaf node must contain ≥5 samples. Prevents overfitting to sensor noise and transient current spikes
bootstrap True Each tree trains on a bootstrap sample (sampling with replacement)
n_jobs -1 Use all available CPU cores for parallel tree construction
random_state 42 + run * 100 Different seed per run: 42, 142, 242, 342, 442

Key Modeling Decisions & Architectural Rationale

# Decision / Assumption Justification
1 max_features='sqrt' instead of scikit-learn's default (1.0 = all features) Critical decision. Scikit-learn defaults use ALL features per split, but the reference used MATLAB's TreeBagger which defaults to sqrt. Without this, trees are highly correlated and RMSE degrades to ~6.6%. Setting sqrt brought RMSE down to ~5.89%
2 min_samples_leaf=5 instead of scikit-learn's default (1) MATLAB's TreeBagger regression default is MinLeafSize=5. This regularization prevents deep, overfit trees and smooths predictions for noisy real-world telemetry
3 Unconstrained max_depth Trees grow fully (pruned only by min_samples_leaf). This allows the forest to capture complex non-linear voltage-SOC relationships
The max_features='sqrt' and min_samples_leaf=5 settings were the single most impactful discovery in this project. Initial runs with scikit-learn defaults (max_features=1.0, min_samples_leaf=1) produced RMSE of ~6.65% — far worse than the reference. Switching to MATLAB-equivalent defaults immediately aligned our results with the reference benchmarks (~5.89%).

Metric Definitions

Metric Formula What It Measures
RMSE √[ (1/n) Σ (ytrue − ypred)² ] Overall prediction accuracy (penalizes large errors)
MAE (1/n) Σ |ytrue − ypred| Average absolute deviation (robust to outliers)
MAX Error max( |ytrue − ypred| ) Worst-case single-instance error
STD DEV σ(ytrue − ypred) Spread/consistency of prediction errors
All metrics are in SOC percentage units (%). An RMSE of 5.89% means the model's predictions are, on average, within ~5.89 percentage points of the true SOC value.

11. Step 11 — Architectural Selection & Baseline Benchmarking

A rigorous engineering evaluation requires justifying why the proposed Random Forest (25 Trees) ensemble architecture was selected over alternative machine learning paradigms, and empirically validating its performance against classical baselines under identical real-world driving cycles.

Why Not Alternative Model Architectures?

Before standardizing on Random Forest, several other widely discussed machine learning paradigms were evaluated theoretically and rejected based on lithium-ion electrochemical properties and automotive Battery Management System (BMS) constraints:

Alternative Architecture Theoretical / Mathematical Limitation BMS Impact & Reason for Rejection
Linear Regression (LR) Severe Electrochemical Non-Linearity: The Open-Circuit Voltage (OCV) vs. SOC relationship of Li-ion batteries features a flat middle plateau (typically 20% to 80% SOC) where terminal voltage changes by mere millivolts while SOC shifts across 60% of its capacity. Linear models assume a constant slope (ΔV / ΔSOC). Fundamental Underfitting: A linear hyper-plane cannot differentiate SOC states across the flat OCV plateau. Furthermore, dynamic current pulses create non-linear overpotentials (polarization resistance, diffusion kinetics) and internal resistance varies exponentially with temperature (Arrhenius dynamics), which linear models cannot resolve without manual feature transformation.
Polynomial Regression (PR) Runge's Phenomenon & Boundary Divergence: High-degree polynomials oscillate violently at the boundaries of the feature space. Real-world Li-ion discharge curves exhibit sharp piecewise “knees” (voltage drops near 0–10% SOC and steep rises near 90–100%) separated by the flat middle plateau. Hazardous Extrapolation & Collinearity: When exposed to unseen operating states (extreme temperatures or high regenerative pulses), polynomial models extrapolate uncontrollably, outputting physically impossible values (<0% or >100% SOC). Additionally, a 4th-degree polynomial across 4 features produces 70 cross-terms, causing severe numerical ill-conditioning and acute sensitivity to sensor measurement noise.
Neural Networks (NN / Deep Learning / LSTM) “Black-Box” Opacity vs. ISO 26262 Functional Safety: Deep networks possess millions of uninterpretable weights and complex activation transitions, making corner-case verification, failure mode audits, and functional safety certification (ASIL-C/D) exceedingly difficult. Heavy MCU Resource Overhead & GPU Dependency: Automotive electronic control units (ECUs, e.g., Infineon AURIX, TI TMS570) run at modest clock speeds (80–300 MHz) with limited RAM (≤4 MB) and lack hardware tensor accelerators. While RF evaluates in microseconds using simple conditional branches (if x <= threshold), NNs demand heavy floating-point matrix multiplications, transcendental functions (tanh/sigmoid), and recurrent state buffers. Furthermore, our 25-tree RF trains in ~19.9 seconds on CPU, whereas NNs require lengthy GPU tuning.

Empirical Baselines Evaluated

To provide concrete experimental evidence, two classical non-neural baselines were implemented in isolated, dedicated pipelines under the exact same 60-trip training split (945,026 instances) and 10-trip testing split (118,974 instances):

  • Decision Tree (Single Tree, max_features='sqrt', min_samples_leaf=5): Serves as an ablation baseline to quantify the exact benefit of ensemble bagging and feature subsampling vs. a single base learner across 5 independent random seeds.
  • K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN, k=5, Distance-Weighted, KD-Tree): Serves as a non-parametric, instance-based distance baseline using StandardScaler feature normalization to benchmark local geometric similarity lookup against tree-based partitioning.

BMS Engineering Takeaways — Why Random Forest is the Clear Winner:

  • Catastrophic Peak Error Suppression: A single Decision Tree suffered a massive peak error of 41.76% on sharp regenerative braking spikes. In an automotive BMS, an estimation error of this magnitude would falsely trigger low-voltage protection, disconnect the main contactor, or strand the vehicle. Random Forest suppresses this peak error to 26.70% (a 36.07% reduction) by averaging out uncorrelated tree errors.
  • MCU Memory & Real-Time Execution Feasibility (vs. KNN): KNN requires storing all 945,026 training vectors (~15+ MB raw float data plus KD-Tree nodes) directly in RAM, and computing multi-dimensional Euclidean distances at every 1 Hz sampling step. Automotive microcontrollers typically have ≤4 MB total RAM. In contrast, Random Forest requires zero training data retention and evaluates 25 compact decision trees via microsecond branch comparisons.
  • Noise Robustness: Current sensor noise and thermal fluctuations are effectively dampened by Random Forest’s bootstrap aggregation, providing a smooth and stable SOC trajectory across complete driving cycles.

Engineering Trade-Off Analysis: Why 50 Trees is the Optimal Choice

The Pareto-Optimal Balance: Why 50 Trees Outperforms 100 Trees for Physical BMS Deployment

  • Negligible Accuracy Delta ($0.0127\%$): Moving from 50 to 100 trees reduces test RMSE by only $0.0127\%$ (4.5734% down to 4.5607% — a minuscule 0.27% relative difference). Multi-seed variation across seeds for 100 trees spans 0.19% (4.56% to 4.75%), meaning $0.013\%$ falls entirely within random statistical sampling noise.
  • 50% Memory & Storage Reduction: An unpruned ensemble of 100 deep regression trees on 945k samples consumes 1.12 GB of storage and active RAM. Selecting 50 trees slashes this to 532.9 MB. For automotive flash storage and memory allocation on embedded microcontrollers, saving over 580 MB is paramount.
  • Halved Real-Time Inference Latency: At a 1 Hz sample rate in an automotive ECU, traversing 50 trees takes half the CPU cycles and generates half the cache pressure compared to 100 trees, avoiding bus contention with critical CAN bus tasks.
  • Elbow Point of Diminishing Returns: 50 trees captures 99.7% of the total achievable accuracy benefit while preserving half the system resource budget.

14. Step 14 — Next Steps & Potential Improvements

The core Random Forest SOC estimation pipeline is complete and matches the reference benchmarks. The following are potential enhancements that could further improve accuracy, robustness, or practical utility:

High Priority (Highest Direct Accuracy Levers on Random Forest)

# Enhancement Description Expected Impact
1 Temporal Rolling Windows & Electrochemical Derivatives Engineer rolling statistical windows (5s, 15s, 30s mean, variance, and min-max spread of Pack Voltage, Pack Current, and Motor Torque), first-order time derivatives (dV/dt, dI/dt) capturing transient electrochemical polarization, and cumulative ampere-hour trapezoidal integration (∫I·dt). Gives the Random Forest temporal memory to differentiate identical instantaneous voltages along flat OCV plateaus without changing the tree model architecture. High: Largest single accuracy lever; expected to reduce RMSE from ~4.57% down to 2.5%–3.2% across dynamic cycles
2 Non-Linear SOC Boundary & Transient Sample Weighting Utilize scikit-learn's native sample_weight parameter inside RandomForestRegressor.fit() to apply non-uniform loss penalties (1.5× to 2.5× weighting) on steep, non-linear OCV boundary regimes (SOC < 20% and SOC > 80%) and high-current regenerative braking spikes (I < -50 A). Penalizes peak errors directly during tree variance minimization without modifying model inference code. High: Directly suppresses the 26%–27% peak MAX error spikes occurring in non-linear operational zones

Medium Priority (Thermal Specialization & Signal Conditioning)

# Enhancement Description Expected Impact
3 Temperature-Regime Stratified Clustering (Arrhenius Specialization) Partition training fleet data across ambient temperature regimes (Cold <10°C, Nominal 10–25°C, Elevated >25°C) with dedicated Random Forest estimators to isolate Arrhenius non-linear shifts in electrolyte conductivity, internal resistance (R0), and charge-transfer kinetics. Medium: Substantially lowers estimation variance and eliminates seasonal drift during extreme cold-weather driving
4 Dynamic Output Post-Filtering (1D Kalman Filter / Exponential Smoothing) Couple the raw Random Forest output with a lightweight 1D Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) or Exponential Moving Average (EMA) time-series smoother. Physical battery cells cannot change state instantaneously; filtering suppresses high-frequency decision tree step jitter and enforces physical charge monotonicity during continuous discharge. Medium: Smooths step discontinuities, reducing RMSE by ~0.2%–0.4% on continuous trips while eliminating instrument cluster jitter

Low Priority (Hyperparameter & Telemetry Calibration)

# Enhancement Description Expected Impact
5 Bayesian Hyperparameter Optimization for 7-Feature Space Execute Bayesian optimization (via Optuna or scikit-optimize) over continuous fractional max_features ratios (0.4–0.8), min_samples_split (2–10), and min_samples_leaf (2–8) specifically tuned for the expanded 7-feature correlation structure. Low-Medium: Expected marginal 0.05%–0.15% RMSE reduction through optimal leaf granularity tuning
6 Rest-Phase OCV Recalibration & Transition Cleaning Implement automated zero-current rest detection (I = 0 A for t > 15 min) in driving telemetry to anchor steady-state open-circuit voltage lookups and filter initial startup sensor noise from the training dataset. Low-Medium: Eliminates initial-condition estimation offsets at the start of driving cycles
