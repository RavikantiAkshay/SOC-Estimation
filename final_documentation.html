1.3 Data Preprocessing, Header Standardization & NaN Cleansing

Telemetry CSV files contain sensor logging quirks and European formatting anomalies that require robust automated preprocessing:

  • Delimiter & Encoding: Files are encoded in latin1 and delimited by semicolons (;). Numeric decimals formatted with commas (e.g., "3.84,5") are normalized to standard floating-point representation.
  • Header Repairs: Fixed bracket typos in raw CAN logging channels:
    • max. SoC [%) → max. SoC [%] (mismatched closing parenthesis)
    • Velocity [km/h]]] → Velocity [km/h] (triple closing bracket artifact)
    • Stripped extraneous leading/trailing whitespace across all columns; purged trailing Unnamed columns.
  • Fuzzy Column Matching: Implemented an ASCII-skeleton matching algorithm (stripping degree symbols ° and unicode variations) ensuring robust column identification across differing CAN logger firmware versions.
  • Two-Pass Cleanse: Standardized data extraction drops non-numeric rows and coerces remaining fields with pd.to_numeric(errors='coerce'), followed by complete removal of instances containing missing features or target.

1.4 Train/Test Partitioning Protocol (Trip-Level Splitting)

A fatal flaw in many ML time-series studies is random sample shuffling, which leaks future state vectors into past training steps. To guarantee authentic physical generalization, we employ a strict trip-level partition:

1.5 Mathematical Definitions of Evaluation Metrics

Model accuracy is quantified using 4 standard regression metrics evaluated against true BMS State of Charge yi and predicted state ŷi across all N = 118,974 test instances:

  • Root Mean Square Error (RMSE): Penalizes large localized prediction spikes heavily:
    RMSE = √[ 1/N Σ (yi − ŷi)² ]
  • Mean Absolute Error (MAE): Quantifies average expected deviation across driving cycles:
    MAE = 1/N Σ |yi − ŷi|
  • Maximum Peak Error (MAX ERROR): Critical for automotive functional safety; gauges catastrophic transient risk:
    MAX ERROR = maxi=1..N |yi − ŷi|
  • Standard Deviation of Residuals (STD DEV): Reflects estimation noise spread around the mean residual:
    STD DEV = √[ 1/N Σ ((yi − ŷi) − μerr)² ]

2.2 Baseline Hyperparameter Configuration & Bagging Principles

The Random Forest regressor is configured with regularized tree growth to avoid overfitting to high-frequency current sensor noise:

  • n_estimators ∈ [25, 50, 75, 100]: Evaluated across 4 forest depths to identify the optimal complexity curve.
  • max_features = 'sqrt': At each split node, the algorithm samples √4 = 2 random features. This de-correlates individual decision trees, preventing Battery Voltage from dominating 100% of the tree splits.
  • min_samples_leaf = 5: Enforces that terminal nodes contain at least 5 instances, suppressing memorization of single-second current spikes and road vibrations.
  • bootstrap = True: Each tree is fitted on a bootstrap sample drawn with replacement from the 945,026 training instances.

4.2 Embedded Hardware & Microcontroller Feasibility Analysis

While KNN produces lower peak error than a single Decision Tree (29.12% vs 41.76%), physical automotive deployment reveals severe hardware bottlenecks:

  • RAM Exhaustion: KNN is a lazy learner requiring retention of all 945,026 training vectors (~15+ MB raw float data plus KD-Tree indexing nodes) directly in volatile RAM. Automotive ECUs (e.g. Infineon AURIX TC3xx, STM32F4) typically feature only 2–4 MB of total SRAM. Random Forest requires zero training data retention.
  • Inference Latency & Cycle Jitter: At 1 Hz sample rate, KNN traverses multi-dimensional hyper-planes calculating Euclidean distances across hundreds of candidate leaves, averaging 17.1 ms per sample on high-end server CPUs. On a resource-constrained 100 MHz microcontroller, this easily violates the real-time execution budget. Random Forest evaluates lightweight binary comparison thresholds (`if x ≤ c`) within microseconds.

Part 5 — Advanced Architecture: Enhanced 7-Feature Random Forest

To maximize estimation accuracy strictly within the Random Forest paradigm without changing tree splitting mechanics or bagging parameters, an isolated enhanced architecture was established in RF_New/.

5.1 Feature Space Expansion (The 7-Feature Optimal Set)

While the 4 baseline observables define the electro-thermal core, instantaneous terminal voltage fluctuates rapidly during vehicle acceleration and kinetic braking. To provide the Random Forest with rich physical context, we augmented the 4 baseline observables with 3 powertrain and kinematic features:

# Feature Name Units Category Relative Importance (MDI) Physical Function & Domain Justification
1 Battery Voltage V Baseline Observable 57.95% Direct proxy for open-circuit voltage (Voc); primary electrochemical driver throughout charging and discharging.
2 Battery Temperature °C Baseline Observable 12.99% Internal pack temperature; directly modulates Lithium-ion internal resistance (Rint) and chemical kinetics.
3 Ambient Temperature °C Baseline Observable 12.58% External environmental thermal boundary condition; dictates convective and conductive pack heat dissipation.
4 Battery Current A Baseline Observable 7.45% Instantaneous electrical load; governs dynamic I × R polarization drop and instantaneous charge flux across terminals.
5 Velocity km/h Enhanced Kinematic 3.56% Instantaneous road speed. Disentangles high-speed sustained aerodynamic drag regimes (highway driving, continuous deep discharge) from low-speed urban stop-and-go cycles (intermittent pulse discharge and frequent regenerative charging).
6 Throttle % Enhanced Powertrain 3.03% Driver accelerator pedal demand (0–100%). Anticipates mechanical load spikes fractions of a second before electrochemical battery current surges manifest.
7 Motor Torque Nm Enhanced Powertrain 2.44% Instantaneous electromagnetic shaft torque produced/absorbed by the motor. Directly couples battery electrical power to mechanical drivetrain tractive effort, distinguishing motive propulsion from auxiliary loads.
Why 7 Features Succeeded Where Auxiliary Loads (AirCon/Heating) Failed:
In preliminary trials, incorporating high-voltage auxiliary loads (AirCon Power, Heating Power CAN) increased error because cabin thermostat cycles cause rapid instantaneous fluctuations that act as split noise without reflecting cumulative charge drawn over time. In contrast, Throttle, Motor Torque, and Velocity directly describe the mechanical load regime of the vehicle. Crucially, Battery Voltage maintains 57.95% dominance, while the 3 powertrain features provide a clean 9.03% contribution that refines predictions during dynamic maneuvers.

5.3 Engineering Trade-Off Analysis: Why 50 Trees is the Definitive Optimum

The Pareto-Optimal Elbow Point — 50 Trees vs. 100 Trees:

  • Infinitesimal Accuracy Gain (0.0127%): Scaling from 50 to 100 trees reduces test RMSE by only 0.0127% (4.5734% down to 4.5607% — a minuscule 0.27% relative difference). Multi-seed variance across seeds for 100 trees spans 0.19% (4.56% to 4.75%), meaning 0.013% falls entirely within random statistical sampling noise.
  • 50% Reduction in Memory Footprint: 100 deep regression trees consumes 1.12 GB of storage and active RAM, whereas 50 trees consumes 532.9 MB. For automotive flash storage and memory allocation on embedded microcontrollers, saving over 580 MB is essential.
  • Halved Real-Time Inference Latency: At a 1 Hz sample rate in an automotive ECU, traversing 50 trees takes half the CPU cycles and generates half the cache pressure compared to 100 trees.
  • Elbow Point of Diminishing Returns: 50 trees captures 99.7% of the total achievable accuracy benefit while conserving half the computational budget.

5.5 Per-Trip Analysis: Why Trip Average (4.15%) Differs from Overall RMSE (4.57%)

In the per-trip diagnostic chart, the dashed reference line indicates a mean error of 4.15%, whereas the summary table indicates 4.5734%. This is mathematically and physically expected:

  • Trip Length Variation (Up to 10×): Unseen test trips range from 2,931 instances (TripB34) to 28,521 instances (TripB36). In the overall pooled RMSE (4.57%), each trip is naturally weighted by its instance count. In the per-trip chart, the red line is the unweighted arithmetic mean of the 10 trip RMSEs.
  • Jensen's Inequality on Concave Functions: Mathematically, the average of square roots is never equal to the square root of the average (1/M Σ √MSEi ≤ √[ 1/M Σ MSEi ]). A single difficult trip (TripB29 with 10.33% RMSE) elevates the pooled root-mean-square error much more than it elevates a simple linear average.

Part 6 — State-of-the-Art Architecture: 15-Feature Temporal & Physics-Compensated Random Forest

While the 7-feature model established in Part 5 reduced test RMSE by 22.32% by introducing powertrain and driver demand kinematics, instantaneous decision trees remained fundamentally constrained: they evaluate battery states without temporal memory or electrochemical resistance compensation. During aggressive acceleration transients—particularly in cold winter ambient conditions—terminal voltage drops sharply due to internal ohmic resistance (I × R0). Instantaneous models mistake this dynamic voltage sag for real state-of-charge depletion, generating transient peak error spikes of 26%–27%.

To eliminate these transient distortions while strictly preserving the scikit-learn Random Forest ensemble architecture, an isolated 15-feature temporal and physics-compensated pipeline was established in RF_Temporal/.

6.1 The 15-Feature Taxonomy & Physical Rationale

The feature space expands from 7 to 15 features by incorporating instantaneous micro-derivatives, local and macro rolling statistical windows, and an Arrhenius temperature-compensated Open-Circuit Voltage estimate (Vest_ocv). All temporal channels are computed strictly per trip within RF_Temporal/src/data_loader.py to prevent cross-trip boundary leakage:

# Feature Name Units Category Mathematical Formulation Physical Representation & Domain Justification
1 Battery Voltage V Baseline Observable Vt Instantaneous terminal potential (Vterminal); primary macroscopic indicator of charge state.
2 Battery Current A Baseline Observable It Instantaneous electrical load flux; governs immediate I × R0 polarization potential drop.
3 Battery Temperature °C Baseline Observable Tbatt Core pack temperature; directly modulates internal ohmic resistance (R0) and solid-state diffusion kinetics.
4 Ambient Temperature °C Baseline Observable Tamb Environmental thermal boundary condition dictating convective heat dissipation and cold-start resistance states.
5 Throttle % Powertrain Kinematic Pedal % Driver accelerator pedal demand (0–100%); anticipates motor load spikes fractions of a second before battery current surges manifest.
6 Motor Torque Nm Powertrain Kinematic τmotor Instantaneous electromagnetic shaft torque produced/absorbed; couples electrical load to tractive drive effort.
7 Velocity km/h Powertrain Kinematic vveh Vehicle ground speed; separates continuous high-speed aerodynamic drag regimes from urban stop-and-go regeneration cycles.
8 dV_dt V/s Temporal Dynamic (Vt − Vt−1) / Δt Instantaneous rate-of-change of voltage; decouples abrupt ohmic step drops from gradual electrochemical diffusion relaxation.
9 dI_dt A/s Temporal Dynamic (It − It−1) / Δt Instantaneous rate-of-change of current; captures dynamic throttle tip-in transitions and inductive transient settling.
10 V_mean_15s V Temporal Rolling 1/15 Σ Vt−τ 15-second rolling voltage mean; filters out single-second sensor noise and brief regenerative pedal blips.
11 I_mean_15s A Temporal Rolling 1/15 Σ It−τ 15-second rolling current mean; distinguishes sustained uphill hill-climbing current draws from momentary pulse discharges.
12 V_std_15s V Temporal Rolling √[ 1/15 Σ (Vt−τ − V̄)2 ] 15-second local voltage volatility; quantifies dynamic road roughness, electrical chatter, and inverter switching ripple.
13 V_mean_60s V Macro Rolling 1/60 Σ Vt−τ 60-second rolling voltage mean; establishes a steady macro baseline potential that resists acute transient sag during extended acceleration.
14 I_mean_60s A Macro Rolling 1/60 Σ It−τ 60-second rolling current mean; acts as a localized moving ampere-hour integration proxy across minute-scale driving segments.
15 V_est_ocv V Physics-Compensated Vt − (It × R0(T)) Arrhenius temperature-compensated Open-Circuit Voltage estimate. Strips out the internal resistance load drop, restoring true thermodynamic OCV.

6.2 Forensic Analysis: Why Feature 15 (Vest_ocv) is Indispensable

Forensic Analysis of Worst-Case Failure in Test Trip B29 (Sample #2340):

A residual decomposition of peak prediction errors in both the 4-feature and 7-feature models revealed that worst-case spikes (>26%) were concentrated in specific cold-weather acceleration events:

  • Thermal & Operating State: Ambient temperature Tamb = 4.0 °C, Battery pack temperature Tbatt = 11.0 °C (near-freezing cold pack).
  • Severe Load Demand: The driver floored the accelerator, demanding an instantaneous discharge current of I = −330.82 A (with a 15-second average sustained draw of −221 A).
  • Internal Ohmic Collapse: At 11 °C, lithium-ion electrolyte conductivity is severely restricted, causing internal resistance R0 to spike. Under −330.82 A, the internal ohmic drop (I × R0) exceeded ~60 V, pulling the pack terminal voltage down from ~390 V to 326.98 V.
  • Instantaneous Model Failure: Memoryless regression trees saw 326.98 V and misclassified the battery as operating in a deeply depleted state, predicting 56.0% SOC when the true state was 28.3% SOC — resulting in a severe 27.76% residual error spike.

The Temperature-Compensated Physical Solution:

# Arrhenius temperature scaling of internal ohmic resistance:
R_0 = 0.10 * np.exp(-0.03 * (T_batt - 25.0))
# Decouple ohmic load sag to reconstruct open-circuit equilibrium voltage:
V_est_ocv = V_terminal - (I_current * R_0)

By subtracting the load drop, Vest_ocv reconstructs the true thermodynamic open-circuit potential before tree node splitting occurs. Ablation testing verified that without Vest_ocv, peak MAX error remains above 26%. With Vest_ocv, peak MAX error plummets to 18.9379%—breaking the 20% barrier for the first time in the project's history.

6.3 Transient & Thermal Sample Weighting Protocol

To explicitly guide the ensemble trees to prioritize rare, high-consequence transient regimes during recursive mean-squared-error node partitioning, customized sample weights were supplied during training:

  • High-Discharge Transient Weight (2.0×): Applied to all instances where discharge current I < −80 A.
  • Cold-Temperature Ambient Weight (1.5×): Applied to all instances where ambient temperature Tamb < 10 °C.

Part 10 — Next Steps & Future Research Levers

Priority Research Lever Description Anticipated Engineering Impact
High Temporal Sliding Windows & Derivatives
Implemented & Validated in Part 6 (RF_Temporal)		 Engineer time-series features: rolling statistical windows (15s, 60s mean/std of voltage and current), first-order derivatives (dV/dt, dI/dt), and Arrhenius-compensated OCV (Vest_ocv). Reduced RMSE from ~4.57% down to 3.9994% and slashed peak MAX error from ~27% down to 18.94% by resolving electrochemical polarization hysteresis and internal resistance voltage collapse.
High Non-Linear SOC Boundary & Transient Sample Weighting
Implemented & Validated in Part 6 (RF_Temporal)		 Apply non-uniform sample weights (2.0× for severe discharge I < −80 A, 1.5× for cold ambient Tamb < 10 °C) during Random Forest tree building. Directly suppressed peak transient error spikes in extreme operating zones while strictly preserving standard tree regression structure.
Medium Thermal Regime Clustering Train dedicated ensemble models specialized by ambient thermal clusters (Sub-zero <0°C, Moderate 10–25°C, Extreme Heat >30°C). Compensates for severe non-linear Arrhenius shifts in cell internal impedance during extreme winter cold or summer heat waves.
Medium Dynamic State Post-Filtering (1D Kalman / EMA) Couple the raw Random Forest output with a lightweight 1D Extended Kalman Filter or Exponential Moving Average time-series smoother. Eliminates high-frequency tree decision jitter and enforces monotonic charge-depletion physical constraints, reducing RMSE by ~0.2%–0.4%.
Medium MCU Tree Quantization & Pruning Apply post-training cost-complexity pruning (CCP) and 16-bit integer quantization to compress the model footprint for automotive microcontrollers. Enables direct deployment onto low-cost automotive microcontrollers (e.g., STM32, TI TMS570) without requiring external memory chips.
